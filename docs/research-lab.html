Research Lab / synthetic visualizations

See the shape of a research problem.

Quantitative research is easier to reason about when uncertainty, disagreement, parameter behavior, and distributions are visible. This lab turns those ideas into interactive diagrams rather than decorative charts.

Important: every dataset on this page is synthetic and illustrative. These scenes are research education, not trading recommendations, forecasts, or claims about product capability.

01 / Strategy Universe

One point is simple. A population has structure.

This first scene is deliberately not a performance leaderboard. It demonstrates how many candidate research objects can occupy several analytical dimensions at once. The axes below are synthetic variables so the visual can teach spatial reasoning without implying a market result.

X · dispersionHorizontal position is a synthetic normalized outcome-dispersion score from 0 to 1.
Y · drawdown axisVertical position is a synthetic normalized drawdown-severity score from 0 to 1.
Z · disagreementDepth is a synthetic normalized in-sample/out-of-sample disagreement score from 0 to 1.
Size · evidence weightPoint size is a synthetic evidence-weight field. Family color groups are illustrative only.
02 / Uncertainty

A single path hides the range of possible paths.

A Monte Carlo view is useful because one sequence can look unusually smooth or unusually poor by chance. This illustration generates multiple synthetic zero-drift paths to make path dispersion visible. It is not a forecast and does not use market data.
