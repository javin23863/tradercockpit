Strategies

Bottom of the 10-day range

This buys a pullback while the market is above its 200-day average. US stock indices have drifted upward over the long run, helped by growth, inflation and monetary policy, and that drift is what a dip-buying strategy leans on. The trend filter keeps it out of a market that is falling for months.

MES (Micro E-mini S&P 500): 99 trades, net 20.1%, profit factor 3.12, win rate 77.8%, max drawdown 2.0%, grade A.
MNQ (Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100): 103 trades, net 15.7%, profit factor 2.04, win rate 74.8%, max drawdown 3.3%, grade A.
MYM (Micro E-mini Dow): 97 trades, net 11.7%, profit factor 1.92, win rate 70.1%, max drawdown 2.1%, grade A.

It was run by the app's own strategy tester on daily bars of the three micro index futures, starting from $100,000, risking 1% of the account on each trade, with 0.01% of each trade's value in costs and a 10% margin. No slippage is taken off. Tested periods: MES 2003-07-13 to 2023-07-11; MNQ 2003-07-13 to 2023-07-11; MYM 2003-07-06 to 2023-07-06. Rules

  • Daily bars. Trade only when the close is above its 200-day average.
  • Buy at the next day's open after a close in the bottom tenth of the last 10 days' range.
  • Sell at the next day's open once the close is back in the top 30% of that range, or after 10 days.
  • Stop: 4 ATR below the close of the signal day. Size: 1% of the account at risk, never less than the market's smallest order.
