Methods/Validation foundations
Method / research controls

Validation is a chain of decisions, not one green check.

Useful validation keeps selection rules, evaluation samples, and evidence identity explicit. These foundations explain what common validation structures can establish—and what they cannot.

Product-neutralEvidence before verdictNo performance promise
Predefined decision rules

Acceptance conditions

Core idea: evaluate a stated metric, operator, threshold, and sample without silently changing the rule after seeing the outcome.

An acceptance condition turns a research requirement into an explicit check: for example, whether a statistic is above, below, or within a specified boundary on a designated sample. The evidential value comes from knowing which rule was active, which data it used, and when the rule was chosen.

What can mislead you?

  • Changing thresholds after reading results turns a confirmation check into another selection step.
  • A passing condition does not establish robustness outside the metric and sample it evaluates.
  • Metric names, cost treatment, and sample scope must stay attached to the rule.
Coarse time partition

Calendar-year consistency

Core idea: partition an observed result by calendar year and inspect whether behavior is concentrated in only part of the history.

Year-by-year checks can reveal whether an aggregate result depends heavily on a small number of calendar periods. Useful summaries include the number of years represented and the share of those years with positive net outcome under one consistent accounting convention.

What can mislead you?

  • Calendar years are arbitrary boundaries and are not themselves market regimes.
  • Short samples or low trade counts per year can make percentages unstable.
  • A profitable year can still contain severe drawdowns, concentration, or fragile subperiods.
Identity and provenance

Evidence and provenance chains

Core idea: preserve enough immutable identity to show exactly which question, configuration, producer run, input, result, and validation record a conclusion refers to.

An evidence chain helps make a research result reproducible and auditable. Stable identifiers, revisions, hashes, or equivalent custody records can prevent later files or runs from being silently substituted for the artifacts that were actually evaluated.

What can mislead you?

  • Strong provenance proves identity and lineage; it does not prove that the research method was economically valid.
  • A complete chain cannot eliminate data leakage, selection bias, or weak assumptions elsewhere in the process.
  • Current mutable system state should be distinguished from historical evidence captured at the time of a run.
Interpretation rule

Keep rule, sample, and evidence identity together.

A validation label is only shorthand. To understand it, retain the exact condition, the sample on which it was evaluated, the metric definition, and the evidence record that identifies the evaluated result.
