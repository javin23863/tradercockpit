How to Read a Footprint Chart: Delta, Imbalance, Value Area

A candle tells you where price went. A footprint tells you what traded on the way: how much at each price, and whether buyers or sellers were the ones crossing the spread.

ChartsOrder flowCoinbase and Kraken
What you need

A crypto chart. No exchange account.

  • A Coinbase or Kraken spot market open in Charts, such as BTC/USD.
  • Nothing else. The footprint is counted from the exchange’s public trade prints, which need no account.
Steps

Turn it on, then read one bar.

  1. Open the market on an intraday timeframe. Minutes to a few hours is where a footprint is readable.
  2. Open Order flow in the chart toolbar and choose Footprint. The same menu has Order Flow (delta and cumulative delta), Bar statistics, Trades and Trade delta.
  3. If the chart asks, press Get prints. It reads the exchange’s recent trades for this chart, which takes up to a minute. On a live chart it then keeps recording and says since when.
  4. Read the rows. Each row is a price. With Display set to Bid × Ask, the left number is what sold into the bid and the right number is what bought at the ask.
  5. Read under the bar. Δ is the bar’s delta: buying minus selling. Vol is its volume.
  6. Find the POC and the value area. The POC is the row with the most volume. The value area is the band of rows that holds most of the bar’s volume, 70% unless you change it.
  7. Find the imbalances. A row is marked when one side is at least three times the other (Imbalance %, 300 by default). Stacked levels marks several imbalanced rows in a line.
  8. Set it up your way. On the Inputs tab: Display (Bid × Ask, Ladder, Delta, Volume or Bubbles), Layout (Cluster or Profile), Row size (automatic, or Manual with Ticks per row), and what shows under each bar.
  9. Set an alert, if you want one. On a live chart you can be alerted when buy or sell imbalance is stacked.
Reading it

Three questions for any bar.

  • Where did the volume trade? A POC at the bar’s high or low reads differently from one in the middle.
  • Who was aggressive, and did it work? Strong positive delta with no progress upward means the buying was absorbed. Strong delta that moves price means nobody stood in the way.
  • Where are the stacked imbalances? They mark prices where one side was in a hurry. What happens when price comes back to them is the thing to watch.
What you should see

Numbers in every bar that has prints.

Every number is counted from the trade prints. Prints that carry no buy or sell side are left out. Bars from before the prints begin have no cells, and the chart says so.

Keep in mind: a Coinbase footprint is Coinbase’s trades and a Kraken footprint is Kraken’s. It is one venue’s view of the market.
Mistakes to avoid

A footprint is evidence, not a signal.

  • Reading one bar alone. Delta means something next to the bars around it.
  • Rows too fine to read or too coarse to show anything. Change Ticks per row until levels stand out.
  • Treating every imbalance as a level. Most are noise. Stacked ones at a turn are worth a look.
  • Forgetting where the prints begin.
Questions

Short answers.

Do I need to pay for a data feed?

No. The prints come from the exchange’s public feed.

Can I get older prints?

Yes, within what the exchange gives. In Data, open Connections, then the exchange’s card, and capture public spot trades for the window you want. Coinbase only reads back a recent window.

What else can I put beside it?

TPO market profile, volume profile, cumulative delta, and an order book heatmap that shows resting bids and offers over time.
