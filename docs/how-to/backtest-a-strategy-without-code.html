How to Backtest a Trading Strategy Without Code

Save a market, enter what it costs you to trade, and press Run in Builder. Builder searches combinations of entry and exit rules, backtests each one on your PC and keeps the ones that pass, so you write no code at any point.

What you need

A paid plan, saved price history and your trading costs

  • Trader Cockpit on Windows, on a paid plan.
  • Price history for one market, saved in the app. Coinbase and Kraken crypto history needs no exchange account, and forex, metals and index history downloads inside the app. Or import your own file.
  • Your commission, spread and slippage on that market.
Steps

From a saved market to a list of strategies

  1. Save a market. Open Data organization, then Connections. Open the Coinbase or Kraken card, choose a spot pair and a timeframe, and capture the candles. On the saved bars, press Use in Builder. Builder takes tick and 1-minute bars up to weekly and monthly ones.
  2. Check the Setup tab. Under Market data, check that your saved market is picked and the dates are the ones you want.
  3. Enter your costs. Set the commission, spread and slippage you really pay. On a crypto market, Use this market also sets starting exits and costs, and the status line lists what it changed.
  4. Choose the search. Pick random, genetic or sequential search, and how long it runs.
  5. Press Run and confirm. The Run tab shows progress and the leaders kept so far.
  6. Open the Strategies tab. It lists what the search kept, each with its numbers, trades and charts.
  7. Read one strategy. Start with Summary: one verdict with the reasons behind it. Then Trades, Equity and By period. On the chart draws every trade on the bars where it happened.
  8. Keep the ones worth more testing. Press Keep as candidate on each one you would test further.
The result

What a finished search gives you

Each kept strategy has a trade list, an equity curve and a verdict. While a strategy has only been tested on the data it was built on, its grade is capped at C and the verdict says why.

A strategy picked out of a large search can look good partly by luck, so treat the list as candidates for a robustness test, not as finished strategies.

Mistakes

Setup mistakes that change the result

  • Leaving costs at zero. The result is then one you could not trade.
  • Testing on so little history that there are only a handful of trades.
  • Taking the top row of a big search at its word. Selection bias explains why.
  • Reading the equity curve without opening the trade list.
Questions

Questions about backtesting without code

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Builder’s rule parts are conditions and price levels, and it combines them for you. If you want code afterwards, the Code view writes the strategy in EasyLanguage, MetaTrader, Pine or Python.

Can I test my own idea instead of a search?

Yes. Start from a strategy template, whose rules are shown in plain lines before you use it, or describe the idea to Apollo and review what it sets up.

Where does the backtest run?

On your PC. Strategies, results and data stay in your own data folder, and searches are not metered.

Which markets can I test?

Crypto from Coinbase and Kraken, forex, metals and indices downloaded in the app, MetaTrader 5 history from your own terminal, and price files you import.
