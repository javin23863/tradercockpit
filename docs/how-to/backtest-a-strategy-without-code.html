How to Backtest a Trading Strategy Without Code

You don’t write the rules and you don’t write the test. You pick the market and the costs. Builder searches rule combinations, backtests each one and keeps the ones that pass your rules.

Strategy BuilderPaid plansRuns on your PC
What you need

A market, its costs, and a paid plan.

  • Trader Cockpit on Windows, on a paid plan. Builder is part of every paid plan.
  • Price history for one market, saved in the app. Crypto history from Coinbase or Kraken is free and needs no exchange account. Forex, metals and index history downloads inside the app. Or bring your own file.
  • What the market costs you to trade: commission, spread and slippage. A backtest without them is a different strategy.
Steps

From a saved market to a list of strategies.

  1. Save a market. Open Data, then Connections. Open the Coinbase or Kraken card, choose a spot pair and a timeframe, and capture the candles. On the saved bars, press Use in Builder. Builder takes bars from 2 minutes to 5 days.
  2. Open Builder. Under Setup, choose Market data and check that your saved market is picked.
  3. Check the dates and the costs. Builder fills in a market’s usual commission, spread and slippage. Change them to what you really pay. On a crypto market it also sets starting exits and costs to suit, and the status line lists what it changed.
  4. Choose the search. Pick how Builder searches the rule space (random, genetic or sequential) and how long the search runs.
  5. Press Run and confirm. The Run tab shows progress and the leaders kept so far. You can leave it running.
  6. Open Strategies. This is what the search kept, each with its numbers, trades and charts. Open one.
  7. Read it in this order. Summary first: one verdict, with the reasons behind it. Then Trades, Equity and By period. Then On the chart, which draws every trade on the bars it happened on.
  8. Keep what deserves more testing. Press Keep as candidate on the few you would test further. Leave the rest.
What you should see

A short list, every trade visible, your costs in every number.

Each kept strategy has a full trade list, an equity curve and a verdict. When a strategy has only been tested on the data it was built on, the verdict says so and holds the grade down.

What this is: a list of candidates. A strategy that came out of a search has been chosen from thousands, so it needs a harder test than one you wrote down first. That is the next guide.
Mistakes to avoid

Most bad backtests fail in the setup.

  • Leaving costs at zero. Small edges disappear at real spread and slippage.
  • Testing on too little history. A handful of trades proves nothing either way.
  • Taking the top row of a big search at its word. The best of thousands is partly luck.
  • Reading the equity curve and skipping the trade list. Look at the trades before you trust a result.
  • Stopping here. A strategy tested only on the data it was built on has not been tested.
Questions

Short answers.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. Builder’s rule parts are conditions and price levels, and it combines them for you. If you want the code afterwards, the Code view writes the strategy out in EasyLanguage, MetaTrader, Pine or Python.

Can I test my own idea instead of a search?

Yes. Start from a strategy template, whose rules are shown in plain lines before you use it, or describe the idea to Apollo and review what it sets up.

Where does the backtest run?

On your PC. Your strategies, results and data stay in your own data folder, and searches have no meter on them.

Which markets can I test?

Crypto from Coinbase and Kraken, forex, metals and indices downloaded in the app, MetaTrader 5 history from your own terminal, and any price file you import.
