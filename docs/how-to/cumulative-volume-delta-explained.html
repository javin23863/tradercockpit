Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD) Explained

Delta is the volume of trades that bought at the offer minus the volume that sold at the bid, inside one bar. Cumulative volume delta (CVD) adds each bar’s delta to a running total that starts again each session, so you can see which side has traded more aggressively since the open.

Get trade prints

Get the trade prints delta is counted from

  1. Open a live Coinbase or Kraken chart. Any timeframe from 1 minute to 1 day.
  2. Choose Footprint as the chart type. If there are no saved prints, the chart offers to read the exchange’s recent trades.
  3. Press Get prints. It reads up to the last 3 hours of trades, which takes up to a minute. The chart then reopens on the market’s saved history.
  4. For a longer window, capture trades in Data organization. Open Connections, then the exchange’s card, and use Capture Coinbase trades or Capture Kraken trades with a UTC start and end.
Add delta

Add delta and CVD to the chart

  1. Press Order flow in the top bar, then Order Flow. It adds a pane with a Delta histogram and a Cumulative delta line that restarts each UTC day.
  2. Or add the CVD indicator. Press Indicators, open Volume & order flow, and click Cumulative volume delta. Its legend reads CVD.
  3. Choose when CVD restarts. Anchor period: Session by default, or Week, Month, Quarter, Year, Each bar, 1 hour, 4 hours and others.
  4. Set the session hours. Full day (UTC) by default. Custom hours take a time zone, a start and an end (09:30 to 16:00 New York until you change them), and whether weekends count.
  5. Choose how it is drawn. Plot as Candles or Line, with a zero line.
  6. Add trade counts, if you want them. In Order flow, Trades shows how many trades made each bar, and Trade delta shows buy trades minus sell trades with its running total.
Reading CVD

What delta and CVD show

  • Delta above zero: more volume bought at the offer than sold at the bid in that bar.
  • CVD rising with price: buying has outweighed selling since the anchor, and price moved up with it.
  • CVD and price moving apart: one side traded more, but price didn’t move its way. Open the footprint of those bars to see at which prices the volume traded.
  • Delta counts the orders that crossed the spread. Orders resting on the book show on an order book heatmap, not in delta.
What can go wrong

When delta and CVD aren’t drawn

  • No prints, no delta: the chart says CVD is counted from your trade prints and this chart has none.
  • Prints with no side are left out, and the legend says how many.
  • A missing first session: if it began before your prints do, it can’t be counted from its start and is not drawn.
  • Bars across midnight UTC, or across your session’s open or close, can’t be split. Choose a shorter timeframe.
  • Renko and other styles not built on time: CVD is counted in time bars. Switch the chart style.
  • Live charts: delta from saved prints is drawn on charts of saved history. Open the market from your saved data.
Questions

Delta questions

Is the side guessed?

No. Each print’s side comes from the trade record. Prints without a side are left out.

Why does CVD start again each session?

So each session is read from zero. Set Anchor period to Week, Month or longer for a longer run.

Which markets work?

Coinbase and Kraken charts with their trade prints, and saved trade files that carry a buy or sell side.
