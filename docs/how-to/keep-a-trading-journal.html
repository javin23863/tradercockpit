How to Keep a Trading Journal

Open Journal from the left sidebar: your paper trades from the app are recorded there by themselves, and you add others by hand or from a broker statement. Then review each trade with a rating, tags, mistakes and notes, and read the reports by instrument, weekday, hour, tag, mistake and playbook.

JournalFree planStays on your PC
Get trades in

Three ways trades get into the Journal

  • Paper trades: fills from your paper account on live prices are recorded under that account by themselves. Bar Replay practice is kept out.
  • By hand: on Trades, Record an execution takes the account, symbol, asset class, side, quantity, price, time in UTC, and the fee and P&L if you have them.
  • From a statement: in Accounts & import, Import statement takes a CSV, HTML, XML, text or JSON file. Pick the account and the statement’s time zone, press Preview import, check it, then Commit reviewed import. Statements from many brokers and platforms are recognised; for any other file, map the Symbol, Side, Quantity, Price and time columns yourself.
Review a trade

Review each trade

  1. Open Trades and click the instrument. Review this trade opens.
  2. Rate it and tag it. Rating 1 to 5, Tags and Mistakes as comma-separated words, and Reviewed when you are done.
  3. Compare it with your plan. Planned stop, Planned target, the Playbook it belonged to, and Adherence: Met, Partial or Missed.
  4. Write the notes and press Save review. Trade notes are free text.
  5. Open it on the chart. Open trade on chart shows the trade’s fills as entry and exit marks on the bars.
Daily routine

Notes, playbooks and routines

  • Calendar & notes: a trade calendar by month and a daily note with attachments. A daily note template fills in the same headings each day. Notes save when you pause typing.
  • Playbooks: a name, a description and a checklist, one item per line. Link trades to a playbook in their review.
  • Routines: before, during or after the market, on a schedule (Weekdays to start), with Mark complete.
  • Notebook: pages with folders, tags, attachments and links to trades.
  • Prop firms: challenge fees, resets, refunds, payouts and renewals, with a cash ledger, the cash return and renewal reminders.
Reports

What the reports show

  • Per account: net P&L, win rate, profit factor, max drawdown and an equity curve.
  • Breakdowns by instrument, direction, weekday, hour, month, tag, mistake, playbook, duration and asset class, each with trades, net P&L and win rate.
  • Export CSV, Export JSON backup, Print / Save PDF and Download PNG summary.
  • Fill matching is FIFO to start, or LIFO or weighted average, in Journal settings.
What can go wrong

Import limits and held-back figures

  • File size: an upload can be up to 12 MB, and one statement up to 200,000 executions. Split a bigger one by date.
  • Several accounts in one statement: when a statement doesn’t say which account each fill belongs to, split it by account first.
  • Re-importing: the same file adds nothing twice. A fill that matches one already saved but with different details is stopped for you to check.
  • Missing contract facts: money figures for a future are held back until you declare its multiplier, and an exact net is held back when fees weren’t reported. The Journal says which.
  • Thin history: the equity curve needs at least two closed trades with known P&L.
  • Date filters: the equity curve and breakdowns use the account’s whole history, not just the dates you filter to.
Questions

Journal questions

Is the Journal free?

Yes. It is on the Free plan. The Apollo drafts inside it need Apollo credits, which come with the paid plans.

Where are my trades kept?

In a file on your computer. Export backup saves the Journal and its notes; account credentials are left out. Restore backup brings it back.

Can I journal backtests?

Yes. Attach backtest adds a saved backtest, kept apart from live and demo accounts.
