How to Robustness Test a Trading Strategy

A backtest shows a strategy worked on the data it was built on. A robustness test tries to break it on everything else. In Workflows the checks run in order, on their own, and each strategy that drops out comes with the reason.

WorkflowsPaid plansRuns unattended
What you need

Enough history to hold some back.

  • Trader Cockpit on a paid plan, and a saved market with enough history that you can keep part of it out of the build.
  • For the other-markets check, a second saved market. Without one, you skip that step.
  • Either strategies you already built, or nothing at all: most ready-made workflows start by building their own.
The checks

What each one asks.

  • Held-back periods (out-of-sample). Does it work on dates the build never saw?
  • Other markets. Is the idea real beyond one symbol?
  • Higher and lower timeframes. Does it survive a different bar?
  • Slippage. Does the edge survive worse fills than you planned for?
  • Monte Carlo. How much of the result was the luck of these exact trades in this exact order?
  • Walk-forward. Does it hold when it is re-fitted on one window and traded on the next? Separate guide.
Steps

Start from a ready-made workflow.

  1. Open Workflows and find one for your market. The library has ready-made validation workflows. Search by market and timeframe, open one and read its steps before you use it.
  2. Press Use this workflow. It makes your own copy of the steps. The library’s original stays as it was.
  3. Move the copy to your market. In Setup, pick your saved market and press Use on every step. Each step keeps its own timeframe where your bars can build it, and tells you when they can’t. Dates land at the same share of your history, with in-sample before out-of-sample. Steps that test another market ask you to choose: use your market for that step, or Skip this step.
  4. Check the held-back periods. On a step, the held-back periods are listed one per row. Add period adds one. Most used configs fills in a standard split, such as Training 50%, Validation 20%, Test 30%.
  5. Check the costs on each step. Same rule as any backtest: your real commission, spread and slippage.
  6. Press Run, then confirm. The steps run in order on their own. Stop ends the run. Run only this step repeats one check.
  7. Read why strategies were dropped. The card “Why strategies were dropped” gives each reason and its share. When a step drops every strategy, the run says how many, why, and that later steps had nothing left to test.
  8. Open a survivor in Results. Monte Carlo shows the results by confidence level and the simulated equity curves beside the original. Walk-forward shows each run’s score. The verdict on Summary pulls it together.
What you should see

Fewer strategies than you started with, and reasons.

A normal run drops most of what it built. That is the workflow working. What is left has been tried on unseen dates, other conditions and worse costs.

Say it this way: “These tests did not break it.” A robustness test can show a strategy is fragile. It cannot show the future.
Mistakes to avoid

How a robustness test gets fooled.

  • Changing the strategy after you see the held-back result, then testing on the same held-back dates. They are not held back any more. Why.
  • Running the checks only on your favourite. Run them on everything the build kept.
  • Loosening a filter until something passes.
  • Reading “passed” as “will make money”.
  • Skipping the slippage step because the backtest already had costs. Fills get worse exactly when it matters.
Questions

Short answers.

How do I know if my backtest is overfit?

Signs: it fails on held-back dates, it works at one exact setting and not the ones beside it, it only works on one market, and it dies when slippage rises a little. Each check in this guide tests one of those.

Can I build my own workflow?

Yes. Add, remove, rename and duplicate steps, turn a step off without deleting it, and save the workflow to reuse.

How long does a run take?

It depends on how much history and how wide the search is. It runs on your PC without you, and nothing meters it.

Does a strategy that passes everything work live?

Nobody can promise that. Passing means you failed to break it with these tests, which is the most a test can say.
