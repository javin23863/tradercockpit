How to Robustness Test a Trading Strategy

To robustness test a strategy, run it through held-back dates, other markets, other timeframes, worse slippage and Monte Carlo, and keep only what none of them breaks. In Trader Cockpit a ready-made workflow runs those checks one after another and records why each strategy was dropped.

The checks

The six checks and what each one asks

  • Held-back periods (out-of-sample). Does it work on dates the build never saw?
  • Other markets. Does the idea hold beyond one symbol?
  • Higher and lower timeframes. Does it survive a different bar size?
  • Slippage. Does the edge survive worse fills than you planned for?
  • Monte Carlo. How much of the result depends on these exact trades in this exact order?
  • Walk-forward. Does it hold when it is re-fitted on one window and traded on the next? Separate guide.
What you need

What you need before you start

  • Trader Cockpit on a paid plan, and a saved market with enough history to keep part of it out of the build.
  • For the other-markets check, a second saved market. Without one, skip that step.
  • Strategies you already built, or none: many ready-made workflows start by building their own.
Steps

Steps with a ready-made workflow

  1. Find a workflow in the Library. In Workflows, search the Library or filter it by asset and symbol. Open a workflow and read its steps.
  2. Press Use this workflow. It makes your own copy of the steps. The Library’s original stays as it was.
  3. Move the copy to your market. In Setup, pick your saved market and press Use on every step. Each step keeps its own timeframe where your bars can build it, and tells you when they can’t. Steps that test another market ask you to choose: use your market for that step, or Skip this step.
  4. Check the held-back periods. On a step, the held-back periods are listed one per row. Add period adds one. Most used configs fills in a standard split, such as Training 50%, Validation 20%, Test 30%.
  5. Check the costs on each step. Enter your real commission, spread and slippage.
  6. Press Run, then confirm. The steps run in order without you. Run only this step repeats a single check.
  7. Read why strategies were dropped. The card “Why strategies were dropped” lists each reason and its share.
  8. Open a survivor in Results. Monte Carlo shows results by confidence level and the simulated equity curves beside the original. Walk-forward shows each run’s score. Summary gives the verdict.
The result

What the run leaves you with

Fewer strategies than the build made, each with a record of the checks it went through. A failed check shows that a strategy is fragile. Passing every check means these tests did not break it, which is not a forecast.

Mistakes

Ways a robustness test gets fooled

  • Changing the strategy after seeing the held-back result, then testing on the same held-back dates. They are not held back any more. Why.
  • Running the checks only on your favourite. Run them on everything the build kept.
  • Loosening a filter until something passes.
  • Skipping the slippage step because the backtest already had costs.
Questions

Questions about robustness testing

How do I know if my backtest is overfit?

Warning signs: it fails on held-back dates, it works at one exact setting and not the ones beside it, it works on one market only, or it stops working when slippage rises a little. Each check above tests one of those.

Can I build my own workflow?

Yes. Add, remove, rename and duplicate steps, turn a step off without deleting it, and save the workflow to reuse.

How long does a run take?

It depends on how much history there is and how wide the search is. It runs on your PC without you.
