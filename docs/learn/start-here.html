A seven-part orientation

Keep the research chain visible.

These are questions to carry through a study, not a recipe for producing a favorable result.

01 · Research question

State the claim before choosing the evidence.

Write down what you are trying to learn and what observation could count against it. A vague goal such as “find a good strategy” makes it too easy to change the question after seeing results.

Why selection changes interpretation →
02 · Information boundary

Separate development information from evaluation information.

Track which observations influenced design, tuning, filtering, or candidate choice. Evidence that remained unused during development answers a different question from evidence that helped produce the result.

Open out-of-sample evidence →
03 · Measurement

Keep definitions, units, costs, and sample scope attached.

A metric name is not enough. Record the producer definition, unit, cost assumptions, position-sizing basis, observation count, and sample so later comparisons refer to the same thing.

How to read result metrics together →
04 · Uncertainty

One realized path is not the whole distribution.

Inspect path shape, drawdown, dispersion, skew, tails, and alternative outcomes under clearly stated assumptions. Uncertainty analysis narrows a question; it does not turn a historical result into a forecast.

05 · Robustness

Look beyond the single point that won.

Ask whether conclusions change under nearby parameter choices, time windows, regimes, execution assumptions, or other justified perturbations. A broad stable neighborhood usually supports a different interpretation from one isolated optimum.

Parameter robustnessNeighborhoods versus isolated peaks.RegimesState definitions before labels.Walk-forwardMove the information boundary through time.
06 · Selection history

Count the search that produced the reported result.

The more candidates, metrics, thresholds, or variants inspected, the more important it becomes to retain that search context. “Best observed” is not the same evidential claim as “prespecified before evaluation.”

Open selection bias →
07 · Evidence identity

Preserve enough context to know what was actually evaluated.

Keep the source, sample, configuration, run, revision, result, and validation identity distinguishable where those records exist. Provenance supports auditability; it does not prove that the method was valid.
