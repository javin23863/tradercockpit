Market regimes in strategy testing

Understand a market regime as an explicit grouping of measured state variables rather than a label pasted onto hindsight.

By the Trader Cockpit team. Educational material, not financial advice.

Read the method

What is it?

Market regimes as coordinates: A regime is a grouping of observations that share defined market-state characteristics. Useful regime analysis starts with explicit variables and rules or a documented model; the label itself is only a compressed description of those coordinates.

What question does it help answer?

Which state variables define the regime, how were thresholds chosen, and does the label remain useful outside the sample that suggested it?

What can it tell you?

  • How several state variables can be viewed jointly instead of one at a time.
  • Which observations occupy similar regions under the chosen variables and thresholds.
  • How changing the definition of stress or direction can change a regime label.

What can it not tell you?

  • A regime label does not predict how long the state will persist or what returns will follow.
  • Thresholds discovered after viewing outcomes can introduce hindsight and selection bias.
  • Three variables cannot represent every relevant feature of liquidity, volatility, trend, or market structure.
