In-Sample vs Out-of-Sample Backtesting: Avoiding Data Leakage

Learn how in-sample and out-of-sample backtesting differ, when a holdout becomes part of selection, and how to recognize data leakage.

Plain-language definition

What is it?

In-Sample vs Out-of-Sample Backtesting: Avoiding Data Leakage: In-sample backtesting uses data that helped develop, fit or select the strategy. Out-of-sample backtesting evaluates it on data withheld from those decisions. A holdout stops being unseen for a later decision when its results inform that decision, even if the file is still labelled out-of-sample.
Research question

What question does it help answer?

What evidence was genuinely withheld from the decisions that created or selected the candidate?

Bounded interpretation

What can it tell you?

  • Whether evaluation evidence was separated from the decisions being assessed.
  • Where a research process risks feeding holdout information back into selection.
  • Why a clean information boundary matters even when the numerical split percentage is unchanged.
Limitations

What can it not tell you?

  • A holdout cannot erase bias already introduced by data cleaning, feature design, universe selection, or repeated experimentation.
  • One successful holdout result does not guarantee stability in later regimes.
  • Calling a dataset OOS does not make it independent if it influenced the process indirectly.
Synthetic example

A holdout becomes feedback.

Synthetic example: a researcher develops a moving-average rule on 2020–2023 data and reserves 2024 for one final test. After seeing a losing month in 2024, the researcher changes the entry threshold and reruns 2024 until the result improves. The first test evaluated the frozen rule; the improved rerun now evaluates a rule selected with knowledge of 2024. Renaming that year OOS does not restore its independence. Record the original result and the change, then evaluate the revised rule on a genuinely untouched later period under a predeclared protocol.

Assumptions

Record what the test assumes.

  • Split time in order and freeze the strategy, parameter search, scoring rule, data preparation and execution assumptions before the final evaluation.
  • Indicators, universe membership, corporate-action data and features must use information actually available at each simulated decision time. Fit preprocessing on development data only.
  • Account for overlapping labels, trades crossing a split and temporal dependence. A chronological split alone does not remove every route for leakage.
  • Record every candidate tried and every consultation of validation data. Data used for model selection is validation data, not an untouched final test.

Read the supporting sources.
