Distribution shape and tails

Learn why mean and dispersion can miss skew, tail mass, multimodality, and other features that change how an outcome distribution should be interpreted.

Plain-language definition

What is it?

Distribution shape and tails: A distribution describes how observations are spread across possible values. Shape includes symmetry or skew, concentration, multiple modes, and the behavior of tails. Tail estimates are especially sensitive to sample size and modeling assumptions.
Research question

What question does it help answer?

What information disappears when a distribution is reduced to one average and one dispersion statistic?

Bounded interpretation

What can it tell you?

  • Whether observations are symmetric, skewed, concentrated, multimodal, or unusually heavy in the tails.
  • How threshold exceedance can differ even when headline scale measures are similar.
  • Which tail or distributional assumptions need explicit validation.
Limitations

What can it not tell you?

  • A histogram shape does not identify the future distribution by itself.
  • Tail frequencies are unstable when the sample contains few extreme observations.
  • Standardization can make scale comparable while hiding economically important differences in units or regime.
