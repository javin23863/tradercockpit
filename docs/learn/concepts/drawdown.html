Drawdown

Understand drawdown as a path-dependent decline from a prior running peak, including depth, duration, and recovery context.

Synthetic examples onlyNo performance promisedResearch concept

Plain-language definition

What is it?

Drawdown: Drawdown measures the decline from a running peak to a later value. Maximum drawdown captures the deepest observed decline, while duration and recovery describe how long the path remained underwater and how it returned to a new peak.
Research question

What question does it help answer?

How far and how long did a path remain below its prior peak, and what does one maximum number hide?

Bounded interpretation

What can it tell you?

  • The depth of losses relative to prior peaks in the observed path.
  • How long an observed path remained below its previous high-water mark.
  • Whether risk occurred as one large valley or several separate episodes.
Limitations

What can it not tell you?

  • Historical maximum drawdown is not a bound on future drawdown.
  • Drawdown depends on the path, sample period, valuation frequency, and starting point.
  • One drawdown statistic does not describe liquidity, tail gaps, leverage, or all forms of risk.
