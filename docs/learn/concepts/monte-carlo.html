Monte Carlo Simulation for Trading Strategies: Uses and Limitations

Learn what Monte Carlo trade reshuffling and resampling can test, how to interpret simulated drawdowns, and why simulation does not prove a trading edge.

Plain-language definition

What is it?

Monte Carlo Simulation for Trading Strategies: Uses and Limitations: Monte Carlo simulation repeatedly generates alternative outcomes under a stated randomization rule. For trading strategies, shuffling or resampling recorded trades can illustrate path sensitivity and drawdown variation. The output describes the chosen model and sample; it does not validate the original trading edge or guarantee future profitability.
Research question

What question does it help answer?

How sensitive is a result to the particular order or sample path that happened to be observed?

Bounded interpretation

What can it tell you?

  • Whether a reported result is unusually dependent on one path ordering under the chosen simulation rule.
  • How a distribution of synthetic outcomes differs from a single headline path.
  • Which percentile or tail questions deserve closer investigation.
Limitations

What can it not tell you?

  • It cannot prove future profitability.
  • A percentile is only meaningful relative to the simulation assumptions that generated it.
  • Naive resampling can destroy serial dependence, volatility clustering, regime structure, or other features that matter.
Synthetic example

Same trades, different drawdown.

Synthetic example: start from $1,000 and use four fixed-size net trade outcomes of +$60, −$40, +$60 and −$40. The ending balance is $1,040 and the largest peak-to-trough drawdown is $40. Reorder the same trades to +$60, +$60, −$40, −$40: the ending balance is still $1,040, but drawdown reaches $80. This additive calculation uses no compounding and already-net outcomes. Resampling with replacement is different: it can repeat or omit a trade and therefore change total profit. Neither operation creates evidence about trades absent from the sample.

Assumptions

Record what the test assumes.

  • State whether you shuffle without replacement, resample with replacement, resample blocks, perturb execution costs or rerun the strategy on altered inputs. These answer different questions.
  • Record the input period, trade count, random seed, number of runs, sizing, leverage, compounding, costs and the exact drawdown definition.
  • Independent trade resampling treats observations as exchangeable. It may erase serial dependence, volatility clusters and the ordering of regimes; block choices introduce their own assumptions.
  • A percentile of simulated drawdowns is conditional on the simulation rule and input sample. It is not a guaranteed loss limit or an unconditional probability of a future outcome.
