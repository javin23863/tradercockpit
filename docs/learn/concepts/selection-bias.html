Selection bias and multiple testing

See why the best-looking result from a large search deserves different interpretation from a result specified before the search.

Plain-language definition

What is it?

Selection bias and multiple testing: Selection bias appears when the same evidence helps choose which result to report. Multiple testing intensifies the problem: as more candidates are compared, extreme-looking results can arise from noise even when no candidate has a true advantage.
Research question

What question does it help answer?

How much of the selected result could be explained by searching many candidates and keeping only the most impressive one?

Bounded interpretation

What can it tell you?

  • Why the number and dependence of tried candidates matter when interpreting the selected result.
  • Why a maximum should be compared with a selection-aware reference rather than treated like a prespecified observation.
  • Where an experiment needs stronger holdout, correction, or resampling logic.
Limitations

What can it not tell you?

  • A simple independent-Normal example does not model the dependence structure of real candidate searches.
  • Counting visible trials alone can miss hidden researcher degrees of freedom.
  • A correction method is only as defensible as its assumptions and accounting of the search process.
