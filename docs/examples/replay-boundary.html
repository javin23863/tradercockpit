Example 002 / replay information boundary

What should disappear when you move a historical replay cutoff backward?

This deterministic example uses synthetic OHLC bars only. It compares an information-safe replay view with an explicitly invalid view that leaves future bars visible.

How to read it

The cutoff changes the admissible information set.

At each position, the safe panel shows only bars whose synthetic interval has completed by that point. The leaky panel keeps the same future bars visible in a different treatment so you can see why a polished chart can still violate a historical information boundary.

Derived information

Anything computed from the future must obey the same boundary.

Indicators, annotations, profile sessions, labels, and summaries can leak information even when future price candles are hidden. A replay-safe implementation needs availability semantics for every layer, not just the base OHLC series.

Limitations

This example teaches information timing, not market behavior.

  • The OHLC sequence is deterministic and synthetic.
  • No trading rule, signal, execution model, or forecast is represented.
  • The leaky panel is intentionally invalid and exists only for comparison.
  • Real chart layers can have more complex availability rules than bar completion.
  • A replay-safe chart does not by itself establish that a strategy test is free of all forms of look-ahead or selection bias.
