# You Don’t Have a Trading Strategy — You Have a Backtest

Operator-facing filming script  
Delivery: operator on camera; no Chatterbox  
Thumbnail: **DON’T TRADE THIS**

Everything under **SAY THIS** is the exact spoken script. Everything else tells you why the scene
exists and exactly what must be filmed.

## Scene 01 — The green line

**TIME:** 0:00–0:45  
**CAMERA:** Operator on camera, medium shot  
**PROP:** Printed green backtest curve  
**DELIVERY:** Direct. Calm. No smile.

### SAY THIS

Open TradingView, and a few clicks can give you a beautiful green backtest.

That green line does not mean you have a trading strategy.

If this is all you have, do not risk real money on it.

What you have is a backtest: a replay of old prices. It asks, “If I had followed these rules in the past, what would have happened?”

Useful? Yes.

Proof it works tomorrow? No.

The gap between a green line and a real strategy is where people trading their own money get hurt. They are not stupid. Nobody showed them what has to happen before the backtest, or what has to happen after it.

That is what we are going to fix.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer believes the green line means the idea is ready.
- **After:** The viewer understands that a backtest is only a replay of old prices.
- **Make them feel:** Challenged, but not insulted.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

- 0:00–0:03: fixed `YOU ARE THE MARKET` ident. No voice.
- Hard cut to you holding the printed green curve beside your face.
- On “do not risk real money,” center text appears: `DON’T TRADE THIS`.
- On “a replay of old prices,” cut to a chart rewinding from right to left.
- Return to a tight shot for “They are not stupid.”

### FILM IT

- Record the spoken section as one A-roll take.
- Leave a full beat after “Useful? Yes.”
- The printed curve is a designed prop. Do not present it as a real trading result.

## Scene 02 — How the green line traps you

**TIME:** 0:45–2:10  
**CAMERA:** TradingView screen, then an `EXAMPLE ONLY` animation, with operator picture-in-picture  
**FIXED EXAMPLE:** `ES1!`, hourly chart, blue fast average, white slow average  
**DELIVERY:** Familiar at first. Serious by the answer-sheet line.

### SAY THIS

You know how this happens.

You open a chart. You add an indicator, which is just a line or signal built from price.

You change how fast the line moves. The example backtest gets greener.

You change it again. The losses get smaller. The result looks smoother. The screen rewards every change with a prettier picture.

It feels like progress.

But you saw the result before you made each choice.

That is like practicing for an exam with the answer sheet open. You may learn something, but you cannot use that score as proof that you mastered the subject.

The old chart already knows how the story ends. Every change you make can quietly fit the rules to that ending.

Be honest. You do not save the ugly version. You save the pretty one. A few minutes later, you remember the winner and forget how many choices it took to create it.

The green line feels like relief. Finally, something works.

That is exactly when you need to slow down.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer remembers changing settings until a backtest looked better.
- **After:** The viewer sees that every change used an answer they had already seen.
- **Make them feel:** Recognized first, then uneasy.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

- Open on TradingView with `ES1!`, `1H`, and the full price axis visible.
- Add a blue `EMA 10` and a white `EMA 50`. Do not show a trading result on this real chart.
- Cut to a deliberately simple panel labeled `EXAMPLE ONLY`.
- In that panel, move `FAST LINE` from `10` to `15`, then `15` to `20`.
- The illustrated curve becomes smoother and greener after each move.
- Keep the discarded settings visible as gray cards: `10`, `15`, `20`.
- On “answer sheet open,” freeze the panel and slide a school answer sheet beside it.
- On “forget how many choices,” zoom out to show all discarded setting cards.
- Return to full-screen A-roll for the final two lines.

### FILM IT

- Capture the real TradingView chart only as the viewer’s familiar starting point.
- Build the changing result as an obvious illustration, never as a claimed backtest.
- Keep `EXAMPLE ONLY` visible for the entire illustrated sequence.

## Scene 03 — Start before the chart

**TIME:** 2:10–3:30  
**CAMERA:** Operator at desk, writing on a large card  
**PROP:** Blank strategy card  
**DELIVERY:** Like you are helping one person beside you.

### SAY THIS

A strategy starts with a written plan. An indicator can be part of that plan, but it cannot be the whole plan.

The plan tells you what must happen before you buy. It tells you when to sell, how much money you are willing to lose, and when to stop using the idea.

It also gives a reason the idea might work.

Traders call that reason a hypothesis. Forget the technical word. It is simply your explanation.

For our moving-average example, the reason might be, “I believe a strong move above the recent average may continue because buyers are still pushing.”

That explanation can be wrong. Good. An idea that can be wrong can be tested.

An explanation that changes after every loss is being protected from the truth.

Here is a quick check. Hand your written plan to another trader. Both of you should place the same trade. If you can follow the same words and do different things, the plan is not finished.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer thinks an indicator is a strategy.
- **After:** The viewer can describe a strategy as rules, a reason, and a point where the idea is abandoned.
- **Make them feel:** Capable of writing the first draft themselves.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

The card fills in while you speak:

- `WHAT MAKES ME BUY?`
- `WHAT MAKES ME SELL?`
- `HOW MUCH CAN I LOSE?`
- `WHY MIGHT THIS WORK?`
- `WHEN DO I STOP USING IT?`

When you say “hypothesis,” show the word for a moment, cross it out, and write `MY REASON`.

### FILM IT

- Film the handwriting from directly above.
- Cut to A-roll for “That explanation can be wrong. Good.”
- Keep the finished card on the desk. Every later scene uses the same card.

## Scene 04 — Make the same idea answer questions

**TIME:** 3:30–5:05  
**CAMERA:** TradingView establishing shot, then a clean line diagram, then overhead desk  
**FIXED EXAMPLE:** Blue `EMA 10` crossing above white `EMA 50`  
**DELIVERY:** Curious. Let each missing answer land.

### SAY THIS

Let us use the same idea for the rest of this episode.

On the chart, a fast blue average crosses above a slower white average, so we buy. A moving average is just the average recent price drawn as a line.

We are looking at S&P futures on an hourly chart. The exact line lengths are written on the card.

Now the questions start.

Do we buy the instant the lines touch, or wait until that hour ends? When do we sell? How much can we lose? What trading fees should we include? What would make us stop using the idea?

The answer cannot be, “I will know it when I see it.” A computer cannot follow that, and another trader cannot repeat it.

That may sound picky. It is. Real money disappears through small details.

So I am not going to invent the missing answers just to make this card look finished.

I am stamping it: not ready.

Call it what it is: an unfinished idea.

That is an honest place to start.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The moving-average rule sounds complete because it gives a buy signal.
- **After:** The viewer can name the missing buy, sell, loss, fee, and stop-using rules.
- **Make them feel:** Curious enough to ask better questions, not ashamed for missing them.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

- Begin with the real TradingView screen from Scene 02 for orientation only.
- Cut to a clean diagram labeled `EXAMPLE ONLY`: blue `EMA 10` crosses above white `EMA 50`.
- On the card write: `S&P FUTURES / HOURLY / BLUE 10 / WHITE 50`.
- Under `WHAT MAKES ME BUY?`, write: `BLUE CROSSES ABOVE WHITE`.
- Leave `WHEN?`, `SELL?`, `FEES?`, `MAX LOSS?`, and `STOP USING?` blank.
- Circle each blank as its question is spoken.
- Stamp the card `NOT READY`.

### FILM IT

- No historical cross or chart date is claimed; the cross is a labeled teaching diagram.
- Film the card questions in one continuous overhead take.
- Keep the pause before the stamp. The viewer should arrive at `NOT READY` with you.

## Scene 05 — Version A and Version B

**TIME:** 5:05–6:30  
**CAMERA:** Operator on camera, then close-up of two cards  
**PROPS:** The same card copied as `VERSION A` and `VERSION B`  
**DELIVERY:** Blunt. This is the behavior you want them to stop.

### SAY THIS

Imagine we fill every blank and call the card Version A.

Version A buys after the fast line crosses above the slow line and that hour ends. It sells when the fast line crosses back below.

Then we run the backtest, and the result looks ugly.

Now the temptation starts.

Maybe we should wait a little longer before selling. That might make the old result look better.

Fine. Test it.

But write the change on a new card and call it Version B. Version A keeps its original result.

You are allowed to improve an idea. You are not allowed to rewrite its history.

Changing the rule after seeing the result is like moving the goalposts after the ball has already been kicked.

Keep both cards. If every losing rule is allowed to change until it looks good, no idea ever dies. A test that can never say no tells you nothing.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer thinks a small rule change can quietly repair the original test.
- **After:** The viewer understands that every changed rule creates a new version.
- **Make them feel:** Caught in a familiar habit, then relieved by a simple fix.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

`VERSION A` card:

- `BUY: AFTER THE HOUR ENDS WITH BLUE ABOVE WHITE`
- `SELL: FIRST HOURLY CLOSE AFTER BLUE CROSSES BELOW WHITE`

`VERSION B` card:

- Copy the same buy rule.
- Change only the sell rule to: `SELL: WAIT ONE EXTRA HOUR AFTER THE CROSS`.

Place the cards side by side. Never erase Version A.

On “moving the goalposts,” use a quick diagram of a ball in the air while the goal moves.

### FILM IT

- Record the entire spoken section as A-roll.
- Shoot a close-up insert of the exact rule change afterward.
- Keep both cards visible for the rest of the episode.

## Scene 06 — Give the backtest a real job

**TIME:** 6:30–8:00  
**CAMERA:** Overhead card and chart strip, then side-by-side screens  
**PROP:** `VERSION A` and a long printed price strip  
**DELIVERY:** Fair. A backtest is useful; it simply has limits.

### SAY THIS

Now Version A can be tested honestly.

The backtest answers a narrow question: how did these exact rules behave on this stretch of old prices?

It can tell you whether the computer followed your instructions. It can expose a buy at a price that was never available. It can show you when normal trading fees erase the result.

It can kill a weak idea quickly. That is valuable.

Suppose the line turns green. Great. All we know is that Version A looked good on the part of history around which it was built.

Next, uncover a different stretch of old prices that stayed hidden while the rules were written. If the idea falls apart there, we learned before risking money.

Then check that the real trading platform sees the same buy and sell moments as the backtest. If one screen says buy and the other screen waits, you are testing different rules.

After that, watch it in the live market with pretend money.

The backtest is the doorway, not the destination.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer thinks the green backtest answers every important question.
- **After:** The viewer sees the separate jobs of old prices, hidden prices, matching software, and a live practice account.
- **Make them feel:** Respect for the backtest, with no false confidence.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

- Put `VERSION A` above a printed price strip.
- Cover the right side of the strip with black paper labeled `DO NOT LOOK YET`.
- Put the green curve under the uncovered side labeled `USED WHILE BUILDING`.
- Remove the black paper only when “uncover a different stretch” is spoken.
- Show two simple screens side by side: `BACKTEST SCREEN` and `TRADING SCREEN`.
- Place the same buy marker on both. Then deliberately shift one marker to show the mismatch.
- Finish on a practice-account screen labeled `LIVE PRICES / FAKE MONEY`.

### FILM IT

- The price strip and green curve are teaching props, not claimed performance.
- Keep the labels large enough to read on a phone.
- Do not show a real account balance or brokerage credentials.

## Scene 07 — Put the whole path in one picture

**TIME:** 8:00–9:05  
**CAMERA:** Overhead desk using the same card and curve  
**PROPS:** Eight large stage cards  
**DELIVERY:** Clear and satisfying. This is the picture coming together.

### SAY THIS

Now the whole path should make sense.

The moving-average rule starts as a rough idea.

We make it answer questions until different people can follow it the same way.

We test the written version on old prices. We reveal the part of history we kept hidden. We make sure the real trading screen sees the same signals. We let it run live with pretend money.

Only then do we consider a small amount of real risk.

And the work still does not stop. We keep watching. If the idea stops behaving for the reason we wrote down, we turn it off.

Look where the green curve belongs. It sits under old prices. Everything to its right still needs an answer.

A green line earns the next question. It does not earn blind trust.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer has learned separate pieces.
- **After:** The viewer can see the order and point to where a backtest belongs.
- **Make them feel:** The process is understandable and possible to follow.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

Place these cards from left to right:

`ROUGH IDEA` → `EXACT RULES` → `OLD PRICES` → `HIDDEN PRICES` → `MATCH THE SCREENS` → `LIVE / FAKE MONEY` → `SMALL RISK` → `WATCH / STOP`

Move `VERSION A` along the path as each sentence is spoken.

Place the green curve under `OLD PRICES`. Leave every later card visibly unfinished.

### FILM IT

- Shoot this as one overhead take.
- Use the same desk position and lighting as Scenes 03 through 06.
- Do not add software names or internal process terms to the cards.

## Scene 08 — What this series promises

**TIME:** 9:05–10:20  
**CAMERA:** Operator on camera with short future-episode montage  
**DELIVERY:** Personal and firm.

### SAY THIS

This is what you are going to see in this series.

We will take a simple rule like this moving-average idea and follow it all the way through.

Before a test, you will see the exact rules we wrote down. You will see which part of the chart we used and which part stayed hidden.

Then you will see what actually happened.

If the idea fails, the failure stays. If it gets through a step, I will tell you what that step showed and what we still do not know.

I am not selling you a secret strategy.

Think about it. If somebody had a rule that reliably printed money, why would they need to sell that rule to you?

What I can show you is how to catch a weak idea before it reaches your money.

A magic indicator gives you excitement. An honest test gives a bad idea somewhere to die.

When you skip this work, the better-prepared trader is trading against you.

You are their market.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer expects another channel selling a trading rule.
- **After:** The viewer knows the series will show the rules, the hidden chart section, the result, and the failures.
- **Make them feel:** Skeptical of secret-strategy sellers and curious about the next test.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

- Keep the exact `VERSION A` card as the recurring object in every montage shot.
- Put a failed card in a red folder marked `STOPPED`.
- Put a surviving card in an amber folder marked `KEEP TESTING`, never `WINNER`.
- Remove all B-roll for the secret-strategy question. Hold on your face.
- Finish the scene with center text: `YOU ARE THE MARKET`.

### FILM IT

- Record the whole section as A-roll before creating any montage.
- The montage covers only the sentences about what the series will show.
- Leave a quiet beat before “Think about it.”
- No product screen, offer, or purchase request.

## Scene 09 — Give the viewer a job

**TIME:** 10:20–11:05  
**CAMERA:** Overhead paper, then tight A-roll  
**PROP:** Blank sheet and thick black marker  
**DELIVERY:** Practical and encouraging.

### SAY THIS

Before you open the testing screen again, take out a piece of paper.

Write this:

“I believe blank will happen after blank, because blank.”

Underneath it, write:

“I will admit I am wrong if blank.”

If you cannot fill those blanks without looking at the green line, close the test.

You have an idea. The work of building a strategy starts there.

In the next episode, I will show you how a backtest can accidentally use information that was not available when the trade happened.

Start with the reason. Write the rules. Make the idea earn the next step.

That is your job today.

### WHY YOU ARE SAYING IT

- **Before:** The viewer understands the lesson but may not know what to do next.
- **After:** The viewer has a sentence to complete before opening another test.
- **Make them feel:** Ready to begin, not overwhelmed.

### WHAT VIEWERS SEE

Write this template by hand:

`I BELIEVE ______ WILL HAPPEN AFTER ______ BECAUSE ______.`

`I WILL ADMIT I AM WRONG IF ______.`

End card:

`NEXT: YOUR BACKTEST MAY KNOW TOO MUCH`

### FILM IT

- Shoot the handwriting in one overhead take.
- Cut to tight A-roll for “You have an idea.”
- Hold the end card long enough to read once.
