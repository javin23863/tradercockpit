## Three checks, stated exactly
# receipt: E03-RW-C03
First, the entry is scored one bar later in the same direction. Latency is the delay between a signal and the fill used to score it. This one-bar view asks how much of the result depended on the original fill arriving immediately. If price moves against the signal during that extra bar, the ledger records the damage; if it moves in the trade's favor, the delayed view records that too. Second, the original trades are split at the median entry hour into session half 1 and session half 2. A split sample is a population divided into declared groups for separate measurement. That asks whether one part of the trading day carried the total. These are three recorded profit-factor gates: one delayed-entry view and two session views. Each view has a transformation, a metric, and a threshold of 1.0.

## 154 becomes 101 and 53
# receipt: E03-RW-C04
The phase starts with 154 candidates. One hundred one fail at least one gate, and 53 pass all three. The gate census records 66 failures on the one-bar delay, 41 on session half 1, and 23 on session half 2. Those failure counts overlap, so adding them would overstate the number of candidates that failed. A candidate that fails two views is still one failed candidate in the headline total. The all-gates rule is deliberately strict: a candidate must clear the delayed fill and both session halves to continue. The only safe totals are the recorded 154 entering, 101 failing at least one, and 53 clearing all three.

## Profit factor is a comparison, not a verdict
# receipt: E03-RW-C07
Profit factor is gross winning dollars divided by gross losing dollars on the same ledger. A value above 1 means the winning dollars are larger for that view. A value below 1 means the losing dollars are larger. The phase uses 1.0 as the gate. For the failed row, 0.986603 means the losing dollars are slightly larger in session half 1, even though the other views are above 1. For the passing row, each displayed ratio is above 1. That single comparison is useful because it is easy to audit, but it still needs the candidate count, the exact transformation, the trade ledger, and the validation boundary beside it. A value of 1.15 is not a guarantee; it is a ratio produced by one specified historical view.

## What this phase can and cannot say
# receipt: E03-RW-C08
This phase can say how the 154 recorded candidates responded to a one-bar entry delay and two session splits. It can show which rows cleared the recorded profit-factor checks. It can show that the surviving population is smaller than the OOS population. It cannot say that the surviving rows will earn money live. The source is real and the wiring is exercised, but validated is still false. A regime is a recurring market condition such as a trend, range, or volatility state. This phase does not identify a regime cause; it identifies sensitivity in the recorded historical test. To make a causal claim, we would need a different design and evidence beyond this phase record. Precision about the limit belongs in the result.

## The fill-and-session worksheet
# receipt: E03-RW-C10
For a timing or execution stress test, write the original population, the exact transformed view, the threshold, the failure count, and whether failures overlap. Keep one failed row and one passing row with their candidate identifiers. Then write the validation status and the next phase. If the clock is used, declare the clock. If the fill is moved, state by how many bars. If a session is split, state the boundary and the rule for assigning a trade. A good result is a relationship that another run can challenge. Keep the 154-to-53 result beside the worksheet. That is how the pipeline turns a vague timing worry into a reproducible research step.
