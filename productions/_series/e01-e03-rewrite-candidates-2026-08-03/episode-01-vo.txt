## What counts as a candidate
# receipt: E01-RW-C03
Before the results, make the idea complete. A candidate is one complete set of trading rules. It names the market and timeframe. It says when to enter, how to fill, when to exit, how much to trade, what risk to accept, what costs to pay, and what history to use. Commission is a fee charged for a trade. Slippage is the gap between the reference price and the fill you actually receive. Change one detail and you have a new candidate. Start with an observation. Write the explanations that could account for it. Pick one and write a falsifiable hypothesis. A falsifiable hypothesis is one that could be proved wrong by a defined result. Here, the development window is the in-sample block. Those are the prices used to build and screen the rule. Net profit is what remains after gains, losses, and costs are combined. Gross wins are the winning trade amounts added together before costs. Gross losses are the losing trade amounts added together before costs. Maximum drawdown, or max drawdown, is the largest peak-to-trough fall in the result line. Return-to-drawdown is the recorded return placed beside that largest fall. Profit factor is gross wins divided by gross losses. How often the strategy won is its win rate. Payoff asymmetry means a smaller number of larger wins can outweigh a larger number of smaller losses. Trade count is the number of trades the rule produced. Trades per month is how often the rule acted. A library records these choices; it stores the search choices alongside the measurements. These are measurements of one window. Live performance remains a separate question.
